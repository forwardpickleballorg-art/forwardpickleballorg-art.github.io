--- title: "SLOW MOTION 101: Watch the Ball, the Body, and the Follow-Through" category: "Training" author: "Forward Pickleball" ---

Elite pickleball happens too fast to learn from. Slow it down, and three things decide every shot — here is how to see them.

Watch a pro rally at full speed and you see a blur of talent. You cannot copy a blur. But slow the footage down and the blur resolves into mechanics — and the same three checkpoints show up on every quality shot, from a dink to a drive. Learn to see these three things in slow motion, and you will start producing them in your own game:

  1. Watch the ball — all the way into the paddle.
  2. Shoulders and body consistency — the same body shape on every shot.
  3. The follow-through — the finish that tells the truth about the whole swing.

Here they are, in the best example there is: Ben Johns and Anna Leigh Waters, the most dominant mixed doubles team in pickleball, in ultra slow motion (from Pro Motion Lab, filmed at the PPA Black Desert tournament in St. George, Utah).

1. Watch the ball — all the way in

Follow the ball into their paddles and watch their heads. Through contact, the head stays down and still; the eyes track the ball right onto the paddle face. There is no early look up to see where the shot went — the result can wait a fraction of a second.

Now think of the average club rally. The head lifts before contact to peek at the target, the paddle face moves with it, and the ball floats or clips the tape. Most "paddle errors" are really eye errors. Slow motion proves it: freeze the frame at contact and check where the eyes are. If the head moves, the paddle moves. Watching the ball all the way in is the first discipline of every clean strike — and it is invisible until you slow the game down.

2. Shoulders and body: the same swing, every time

Watch their shoulders. On every shot, the shoulders turn early to prepare, stay level through the swing, and the body stays balanced — no lunging, no falling away, no spinning off the shot. Now compare three forehands in a row. The body shape is identical each time. That is not a coincidence; it is the whole secret. A consistent body produces a consistent ball.

Amateur swings fail this test. One forehand is all arm, the next is a lean and a reach, the third is hit falling backwards. Three different body shapes, three different results — and the player blames the paddle. Slow motion lets you compare shot to shot and see the truth: the pros are not doing anything magical. They are doing the same thing, every time. Consistency of the shoulders and body is the skill. Everything else is decoration.

3. The follow-through tells the truth

A swing does not end at contact. Watch where their paddles finish: through the target line, smooth and complete — the follow-through flowing in the direction the ball was sent, finishing in the same place for the same shot, every time.

The follow-through is the signature of the whole swing. A short, jabby, cut-off finish means the ball was pushed, and pushed balls sit up. A full, controlled finish means the ball was struck cleanly, with shape and purpose. Here is the useful part: the follow-through is the easiest piece to copy. Imitate the pros' finish — same height, same direction, same calm — and the middle of your swing quietly reorganizes itself to get there. Slow motion is how you learn what that finish actually looks like.

How to study: three passes, ten minutes

  1. Ball pass. Quarter speed. Eyes on the ball and the striker's head. Freeze at contact — where are the eyes?
  2. Body pass. Same rally. Shoulders and body only. Is the shape the same on every shot? (On YouTube, step frame by frame with the comma and period keys.)
  3. Follow-through pass. Watch only the finishes. Same shot, same finish?

Ten minutes of this beats an hour of highlights. Highlights entertain; study teaches.

Then turn the camera on yourself

Prop your phone on the fence — most phones shoot true slow motion — record one game, and run the same three checks on your own footage. Head still at contact? Shoulders level and the body shape repeatable? Follow-through complete and consistent? Whatever fails the test is your next drilling target, already identified. That is deliberate practice: zero wasted rallies, because every rally on film tells you exactly what to fix.

Watch the ball. Keep the body the same. Finish the swing. Slow motion shows you all three — then your training has targets.
