--- title: "The Forehand Drive: Different Styles, Same Fundamentals" category: "Training" author: "Forward Pickleball" redirect_from: - /blog/2026/11/24/forehand-drive-fundamentals/ ---

Two players can hit forehand drives that look nothing alike — and both can work. What matters is not the style, it is the fundamentals underneath. Three of them show up in every good drive, whatever the style: early preparation, a compact takeback, and a consistent contact point.

1. Preparation: split step and turn

Split step right before your opponent makes contact with the ball. The moment you see the ball come off their paddle, you know which side it is going to — so start setting up right away. There is no excuse for preparing late.

The split step is followed by a unit turn: shoulders and hips turn together, and that turn is what sets up a consistent contact point. The turn matters more than the stance — a closed stance, a semi-open stance, or an open stance can all work, as long as the shoulders and hips are turned.

2. Takeback: keep it small

The takeback does not need to be a big swing. Split step and turn — that is the whole setup. If you pull the paddle back first and then turn, the paddle ends up too far behind you.

Keep the paddle and your hand on the hitting side of your body, so the swing stays compact. A big loop with the elbow held high is a tennis habit — it works for the player who grew up with it, but it is not the model to copy.

3. Timing and contact point: slightly out in front

Contact the ball slightly out in front of you, around your front knee. The range that works is from a little behind the knee to a little in front — everyone is a bit different, so there is room for your own spot. Too far back, or way too far out in front, and you lose your rhythm.

Keep your weight forward. Too many players hit this shot on their back foot or off balance. Hit it out in front with your weight moving forward, so after a good one you can follow it straight in — and after a poor one, back up and wait for the next ball.

4. Footwork: open up and step in

After the split step, the right leg (for right-handed players) opens out to the side, so you can step out in front and get behind the ball. That is the default. Stepping back is for the occasional fast or deep ball, not the default.

From there you can also step in with the left leg. Load your legs and hips as you set up, then let the hitting hip drive forward toward the net as you make contact.

5. The goal of the drive: keep it low

The goal of a drive is not to hit the ball as hard as possible. It is to keep the ball low and get it to your opponent fast, so they have to hit from low to high. The payoff is a pop-up you can put away — the drive is a pressure shot, not a power winner.

6. Stillness at contact

Keep your upper body quiet and your head still through contact. Extra movement is wasted movement. Follow through over your shoulder.

Putting it together, step by step

  1. Be in ready position.
  2. Split step. Recognize it is a forehand and start the setup — open the hip with the turn and start moving toward the ball.
  3. Go toward the ball.
  4. Contact it in front of you, with control — keep it low, make it dip, make your opponent struggle.
  5. Read the shot. If you hit it well, follow it in. If not, back up, wait for the next ball, and do it again.

Styles differ. Fundamentals do not. Work on the fundamentals, and find the style that fits you.
