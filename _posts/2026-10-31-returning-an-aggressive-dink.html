When returning an aggressive dink, the choice between down the line and diagonally (crosscourt) depends on three things: your contact point, your opponent’s position, and how much time you have.

My general recommendation for 3.5–4.0 players is:

Use crosscourt as your default recovery shot, but use down the line when you have a clear tactical advantage.

1. Crosscourt vs. down the line

Crosscourt dink

Good default option

  • Longer diagonal distance gives more margin over the net.
  • Allows a lower-risk return.
  • Can move your opponent away from the middle.
  • Gives you time to recover and reposition.

Down-the-line dink

  • Useful when your opponent is cheating toward the middle.
  • Can target their outside foot or backhand.
  • Changes the direction of the rally.
  • Requires better accuracy because the distance is shorter.

2. Five tips when defending an aggressive dink

  1. Don’t rush the return. Let the ball come into your hitting zone. Avoid reaching and making contact too early.
  2. Use a compact backhand block. For many players, the backhand is a reliable way to absorb pace because it allows a relatively stable paddle position in front of the body.
  3. Aim for your opponent’s feet. Instead of trying to hit a winner, make your opponent bend and hit another ball upward.
  4. Change direction carefully. If your opponent attacks crosscourt, you don’t always need to return crosscourt. A controlled down-the-line dink can disrupt their rhythm.
  5. Recover after contact. Don’t admire your shot! Return to a balanced ready position and anticipate the next attack.

3. My recommended decision-making guide

Situation Recommended response
Under pressure, low contact Soft crosscourt block
Opponent leaning toward middle Down the line
Opponent attacking your backhand Compact backhand reset
Ball sits up Controlled counterattack
Opponent far outside court Dink into open space
Difficult ball near your feet Reset into kitchen

One coaching principle I particularly like

Don’t return the ball to where your opponent is strongest. Return it to where your opponent is least prepared.

At the 3.5–4.0 level, many players become predictable because they automatically dink crosscourt.

Teach them to read the opponent’s body position, paddle position and balance before choosing their return direction.

That’s the difference between simply playing a dink rally and actually playing tactical pickleball.
