Five letters, one place to play: COURT. Carry these five ideas onto court with you, and you will make better decisions in every rally — not just hit better shots.

C — Consistency

Get the ball in, again and again. A reliable serve, a reliable return, a reliable dink, and a reliable third shot put pressure on your opponents without you having to hit a winner. When you are consistent, your opponents have to beat you. Most points at the 3.0 to 4.0 level are decided by who keeps the ball in play longer, not by who hits the hardest shot.

Try it: in your next game, count how many balls you put in play in a row. Aim for one more than your last rally, every rally.

O — Opportunities

Watch for the ball you can actually do something with: a ball that sits up, an opponent caught out of position, a short reply, a ball at your favourite height. An opportunity is not every ball — it is the right ball. Spotting it early is a skill, and it starts with watching your opponent, not just your own shot.

Observing: watch their feet, their paddle, and their position between shots. A player stepping back from the kitchen line, a paddle dropping low, a player leaning the wrong way, or a pair leaving a gap in the middle — those are signs an opportunity is coming. Watch patterns, too: where do they like to dink, where do they struggle to reset, and what do they do under pressure? The more you observe, the earlier you see the right ball.

Creating: do not just wait for an opportunity — create one. Move your opponent with placement: a wide dink to pull them out of position, a deep ball to push them back, a soft ball to draw them in, then a firm ball into the space they just left. Change pace and angle, target a weaker side, and use the middle to make a pair hesitate over who takes it. A well-placed shot before the attack is often what makes the attack possible.

Try it: before each rally, pick one cue to watch for, like a high ball or a player stepping back from the kitchen line. When you see it, you will know it. Then pick one way to create the next one — for example, two wide dinks to open the middle, then attack the middle.

U — Unity

Doubles is a pair, not two singles players sharing a court. Move together, cover the middle together, and talk to each other — who takes the middle ball, when to move up, when to hold. When one player moves, the other moves with them, so there is no gap to attack. Unity also means lifting your partner up after a tough rally, not going quiet.

Try it: agree on one call before the game, like “mine” for the middle, and use it every time. Small, clear talk changes a whole game.

R — Reset, Rhythm & Resilience

R is three ideas in one, and they work together. Reset first: after an attack or a tough ball, play a soft reset into the kitchen and reset the rally instead of forcing a bad shot. A good reset buys you time and puts you back in the point.

Rhythm second: keep your own rhythm, and do not let your opponent dictate it. Stay in a steady dink rhythm when you need to settle, then break that rhythm when you are ready to change the point. Rhythm is your timing inside a rally — steady when you need it, broken when it helps you.

Resilience third: patience under pressure. Stay in the rally, stay balanced, and stay ready after a tough point. A resilient player does not give the point away after a bad bounce or a missed shot — they reset, get their paddle up, and play the next ball well. Points are often won by the pair that recovers fastest, not the pair that started best.

Try it: after any rally, take one breath, reset your feet, find your rhythm again, and focus only on the next ball. The last point is done — the next one is yours to play.

T — Tempo

Tempo is patience, upgraded. Patience alone can mean waiting. Tempo means you control the pace: slow the rally down with soft dinks and resets when you need to settle, then speed it up when the ball sits up or your opponent is out of position. Change the rhythm, and your opponent cannot settle into theirs.

Tempo is not about playing fast all the time. It is about choosing when the point changes — a soft ball to buy time, a firm ball to take time away, a pause in a dink rally before the speed-up. You are still patient, but now you are patient with a plan.

How the five letters fit together

Consistency keeps you in the rally. Unity keeps you connected as a pair. Reset, Rhythm & Resilience keep you steady when it gets tough — reset the point, find your rhythm, and recover for the next ball. Opportunities show you when to go, and Tempo decides when — and at what pace.

Put simply: stay in, stay together, reset, find your rhythm, spot it, and control the pace of it.

Put it into your game

Pick one letter for your next game. Play a whole game focused only on Unity, then a game focused only on Tempo, and see which one changes your score most. Then stack them: consistency first, tempo second.
