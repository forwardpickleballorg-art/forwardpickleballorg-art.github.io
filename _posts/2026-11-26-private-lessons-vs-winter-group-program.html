Take a winter group program open for registration in 2026 as an example: ten weeks, six players per coach, NCCP Certified coaches, $250. At first glance, that looks cheap: 10 sessions of 1 hour 45 minutes is 17.5 hours of court time, which works out to about $14 an hour.

But court time is not what you are buying. Coach time is. And that is where the value flips.

The math: what an hour of actual coaching costs

In a group of six, the coach's attention is split six ways. In a 105-minute session, each player gets about 17.5 minutes of direct coaching. Over the whole 10-week program, that adds up to about 175 minutes — just under 3 hours of direct coaching per player. So the $250 buys about 3 hours of a coach's attention, which is roughly $86 per hour of direct coaching.

Put another way: the same 175 minutes of direct coaching the group program gives you over ten weeks costs $131 in private lessons, $146 semi-private, or $175 in a group of four. One single private hour — 60 minutes of undivided coaching — is worth about three and a half group sessions of direct coaching.

What else the money buys — or does not

Your game, or the average game. The group program covers a wide range, from Novice Level 1 to Intermediate 1. Drills in a mixed class are pitched to the middle, so the stronger players wait and the weaker players struggle. A private lesson is built around your game: your shots, your footwork, your mistakes, fixed on the spot.

Your schedule, or theirs. The program runs on fixed Saturdays. Miss a Saturday and that session is gone. Private lessons are booked when it suits you, and you pay per session — no $250 commitment up front.

Waiting, or playing. In a group of six, most of the 105 minutes is spent watching the other five players. In a private hour, every rally is yours, and every rally is coached.

The honest trade-off

The group program does give you something private lessons do not: 17.5 hours of court time with other players to hit with, at a low price per hour. If cheap court time and a fixed Saturday routine is what you want, it is a fair deal.

But if you want coaching — a coach watching your shots, correcting your mechanics, and building your game — private lessons give you the same amount of a coach's attention for about half the money, aimed entirely at you. That is the better value.
