--- title: "Why Beginners Don't Use the Elbow" category: "Beginners" author: "Forward Pickleball" redirect_from: - /blog/2026/11/15/why-beginners-dont-use-the-elbow/ ---

Watch a beginner swing and you will see it: the whole arm moves as one stiff unit, driven from the shoulder, elbow locked. The hinge is there, but they never use it. A few reasons stack on each other.

Nobody told them the elbow is a hinge

Their mental model is "hit the ball with the paddle," so the whole arm moves as one piece. The idea that the arm is two levers joined at the elbow just is not in their picture yet — you cannot use a tool you do not know exists.

They think power comes from the arm

So they wind up a big arm swing. A big swing with a locked elbow feels powerful, and sometimes it is — once. But the elbow never folds, so there is no hinge, no lever, and no repeatability. The power they feel is the shoulder muscling through, not the core driving a lever.

They are late, so they brace

Beginners read the ball late. When the ball is already on them, the body stiffens and the elbow locks — it is a bracing reflex, not a swing. The hinge only works when there is time to set it: elbow in front, slightly bent, before the swing starts. Late means reaching, and reaching means a straight arm.

The wrist takes over instead

When the elbow does not hinge, something else has to create the paddle movement, and it is usually the wrist. That is why beginners flick — and why their balls pop up or spray. The wrist is doing the elbow's job, badly.

Grip too tight

A death grip locks the wrist and elbow together, so the whole arm becomes one rigid stick. A hinge cannot hinge when everything around it is clenched.

No shoulder turn, so nothing to hinge against

The elbow hinge works because the shoulder turn and core rotation drive it. Beginners often stand square and swing the arm alone, so the elbow never gets loaded in the first place — there is no turn for it to unload from.

They cannot feel it yet

The hinge is a small, subtle movement, and beginners are watching the ball, not their body. They do not have the feel for what a loaded elbow versus a locked elbow even is.

What it looks like on court

Big backswings, arm-only shots, wrist flicks, balls into the net or sailing long, and sore shoulders and elbows — because the joints are absorbing force the core should be handling.

The fix is small on purpose

Keep the elbow in front and slightly bent, loosen the grip, and practise short swings — dinks are perfect for it — until the forearm swinging on the hinge starts to feel normal. Once they feel it once, they start using it everywhere.

The takeaway: beginners do not use the elbow because nobody showed them the hinge, and everything about being late, tight, and arm-focused keeps it locked. Show them the hinge, give them time to set it, and the elbow starts doing its job.
