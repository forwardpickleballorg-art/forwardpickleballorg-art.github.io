You can have great shots, quick hands, and plenty of power — and still lose points because you are standing in the wrong place. The Step of Champions is one small move that fixes that: a single step toward the middle, at exactly the right moment.

What it is

When your partner drives the ball down the line, take one step toward the middle of the court. Just one step. You are scooting over to look for the next ball, ready to be aggressive.

Why it works

Your partner’s drive puts pressure on your opponents. Your step takes some of that pressure off your partner — because now you are the one in position to take the next ball. If your opponents leave something up, you are right there to attack it. If the ball comes back a little slower, you can take it and help your team work its way up to the kitchen line together.

It also sets up the shake and bake: your partner creates the pressure with the drive, and your position lets you finish the point on the response.

The trigger: watch where the drive goes

The step only works if you recognize where your partner’s drive is going. If it goes down the line, take the step toward the middle. That is the whole read — one step, one direction, at the moment the drive goes down the line.

How it pairs with the two-step cheat

The two-step cheat comes first. While your partner is hitting the third ball, you take two steps forward into the court — before you know whether it is going down the line or crosscourt. Once you see where it is going, you adjust: if it goes down the line, slide toward the middle and take the Step of Champions. If it goes crosscourt, stay out and cover your line.

Respect the shot

None of this works blindly. If your partner’s drive is weak or high, do not step in and leave the court exposed. The step follows a good shot — read the quality and the direction of your partner’s ball first, then move.

The takeaway: one step toward the middle, when your partner drives down the line, puts you in position to take the next ball, take pressure off your partner, and attack anything left up. Small step, big difference.
