Most players try to get more power from their arm. But power in pickleball starts at the ground — in your toes, and in how your weight moves through the shot.

Stay on your toes

In the ready position, your weight should sit on the balls of your feet, heels light. That is what lets you move in any direction the moment the ball is hit. Flat feet keep you stuck: by the time your heels lift and you start moving, the ball is already past you.

It is the same idea as the ready stance — nose roughly over toes, knees soft, weight forward. You are not standing on your toes like a dancer. Your heels just stay light enough that the first step is instant.

Weight transfer is the power

Every good shot moves weight from the back foot to the front foot. You load the back foot as the ball comes to you, then transfer forward through contact. That forward movement is what gives the ball pace and depth — not a bigger arm swing.

Hit off the back foot, leaning away from the ball, and the shot goes weak or floats up. Transfer forward and the same swing produces a heavier, lower ball.

The sequence, in order

It all happens in one flow:

  1. Split-step on your toes as your opponent hits, so you land light and ready.
  2. Move to the ball with small, quick steps, staying on the balls of your feet.
  3. Plant and load the back foot as you set up for the shot.
  4. Transfer forward through contact, weight moving onto the front foot.
  5. Recover back to the balls of your feet, ready for the next ball.

What goes wrong

Most weight-transfer problems come from the feet stopping too early. Weight stuck on the heels means late movement and arm-only shots. Feet planted flat means no load on the back foot, so there is nothing to transfer. And leaning back at contact sends the weight the wrong way — away from the ball instead of through it.

The takeaway: stay on your toes so you can move, and let your weight travel forward through every shot. The arm guides the ball. The feet and the weight transfer give it everything else.
