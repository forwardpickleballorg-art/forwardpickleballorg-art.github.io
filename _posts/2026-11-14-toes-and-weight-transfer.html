--- title: "Toes and Weight Transfer: Your Feet Set It Up, Your Core Powers It" category: "Training" author: "Forward Pickleball" redirect_from: - /blog/2026/11/14/toes-and-weight-transfer/ ---

Most players try to get more power from their arm. But real power comes from your core — your hips and trunk rotating through the shot. Your toes and your weight transfer set that up: they get you into position and load the shot, so the core has something to work with.

Stay on your toes

In the ready position, your weight should sit on the balls of your feet, heels light. That is what lets you move in any direction the moment the ball is hit. Flat feet keep you stuck: by the time your heels lift and you start moving, the ball is already past you.

It is the same idea as the ready stance — nose roughly over toes, knees soft, weight forward. You are not standing on your toes like a dancer. Your heels just stay light enough that the first step is instant.

The core is the power

Weight transfer moves you forward into the shot: you load the back foot as the ball comes to you, then shift forward through contact. That forward movement sets the shot up — but on its own it does not create much pace. The power comes from your core: hips and trunk rotating through contact. The weight transfer loads it, the core uncoils, and the arm mostly guides the ball.

Hit off the back foot, leaning away from the ball, and the shot goes weak or floats up. Transfer forward and rotate the core, and the same swing produces a heavier, lower ball.

The sequence, in order

It all happens in one flow:

  1. Split-step on your toes as your opponent hits, so you land light and ready.
  2. Move to the ball with small, quick steps, staying on the balls of your feet.
  3. Plant and load the back foot as you set up for the shot.
  4. Transfer forward and rotate the core through contact, weight moving onto the front foot.
  5. Recover back to the balls of your feet, ready for the next ball.

What goes wrong

Most weight-transfer problems come from the feet stopping too early. Weight stuck on the heels means late movement and arm-only shots. Feet planted flat means no load on the back foot, so there is nothing to transfer. And leaning back at contact sends the weight the wrong way — away from the ball instead of through it.

The takeaway: stay on your toes so you can move, let your weight travel forward to load the shot, and let your core deliver the power. The arm guides the ball — the core gives it everything else.
