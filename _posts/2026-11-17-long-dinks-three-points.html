--- title: "The Return: Three Points That Make It Stable" category: "Beginners" author: "Forward Pickleball" redirect_from: - /blog/2026/11/17/long-dinks-three-points/ ---

A return comes in at medium speed or above, so you have to move quickly to meet it. Three points make it stable: use your feet to find the ball, shift your weight forward and wait for the bounce, and brush the ball up and out to create topspin.

1. Footwork first: use your feet to find the ball

The first basic point is footwork. First use your feet to find the ball — get to it and have a foot behind the incoming ball before you play it, instead of standing still and reaching for it.

The common mistake is reaching out with the arm while the body stays upright. Then your centre of gravity is already almost out of balance, and it is very hard to use the power of your body to push the ball over. Move your feet first, get behind the ball, and the shot has a base to work from.

2. Shift your weight forward, paddle in front, and wait for the bounce

The second point is the centre of gravity going forward. Keep the paddle in front of your body as your weight shifts forward, in a low stance with your knees bent.

Then wait for the ball to land and bounce up toward you — and only then, from the bottom of the ball, lift upward. Through that weight transfer you return to the middle, ready for the next ball.

3. Do not wrist it: brush up and out for topspin

Two mistakes ruin returns. The first is hitting the bottom of the ball with the wrist: a wrist flick sends the ball flying and out of control, and it is the most common problem. The second is lifting too high on the follow-through, even though the paddle starts low.

The correct way is a return with topspin. When you lift gently up and out, brush the ball from its bottom, outward and parallel outward — that friction produces topspin. Think of it as a waving-hello gesture: hand up, palm out. It is not pulling the ball like a drive. Many professional players use topspin on the return, and that brushing lift is what lets the ball carry over and dip into the court.

The takeaway: feet first to find the ball, weight forward with the paddle in front, wait for the bounce, then brush up and out from the bottom of the ball. Three small points, and the return goes from shaky to stable.
