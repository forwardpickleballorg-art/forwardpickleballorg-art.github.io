Drill 01 · Soft Game · 4 Players

The Four-Corner Return Rotation

All four players hold their position at the strike zone. The ball moves in a continuous rotation — straight across, then diagonal — so every player works both angles.

Drill Sequence

1Player A returns straight across to Player B

2Player B returns diagonally to Player C

3Player C returns straight across to Player D

4Player D returns diagonally back to Player A

Every player stays anchored at the strike zone for the full rotation — alternating straight and diagonal returns builds soft hands, patience, and control from both angles.

Why Run This Drill

ObjectiveGroove consistent, controlled soft returns from the strike zone — keeping every ball low and unattackable while the target keeps changing.

Master both angles. Alternating straight and diagonal returns trains the two placements you use most in real rallies — no angle becomes a weakness.

Quicker recovery between shots. The ball arrives from a different direction each time, so you learn to reset your paddle and posture immediately after every return.

Patience under rhythm. The continuous rotation rewards steady, repeatable contact over risky winners — the habit that wins long soft-game exchanges.

Four players, zero standing around. Everyone touches the ball every rotation, so all four players get equal reps in a single feed.

Drill 02 · Attack · 3- 4 Players

Force the Pop-Up, Finish the Point

Steady pressure creates weak balls. This three-step sequence trains your team to force the pop-up — then step in and end the rally.

Drill Sequence

1Player C drives a firm, deep return at Player B — pressure the body or backhand

2Under pressure, Player B pops the ball up short over the net

3Player A steps in and smashes into the open court that B left behind

Rotate roles each rally so every player practices both sides: applying pressure and finishing, and absorbing pressure without popping the ball up.

Why Run This Drill

ObjectiveTurn pressure into points — learn to create the weak ball on purpose, recognize it instantly, and finish it before the defense recovers.

Purposeful pressure. Targeting the body or backhand teaches you that a well-placed return — not a harder one — is what forces errors.

Faster pop-up recognition. Repetition trains your eyes to read a rising ball early, so you’re already moving forward while opponents are still reacting.

A confident put-away. Stepping in and smashing to the open court builds the finishing instinct many players hesitate on in matches.

Better defense, too. The player under pressure gets equal value: learning to absorb pace and keep the ball low instead of lifting it.

Got a Fifth Player?

5 Players The cleanest fix is a rotate-on-error system. The fifth player waits at one designated corner (say, behind Player A’s baseline). Whenever someone puts a ball into the net or pops it up high, they step out, the waiting player steps into their spot, and the rally restarts immediately. This actually improves the drill — it adds a light consequence for errors, which keeps everyone’s focus sharp without turning it into a competitive game. Alternatively, you can rotate on time: every 2 minutes, one player out, fifth player in, so reps stay roughly equal.
