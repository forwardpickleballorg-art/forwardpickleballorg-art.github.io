Drive, drop & reset: two drills to move past 3.5

Stuck at 3.5? These two drills cover the baseline and the transition zone — changing gears from a hard drive to a soft drop, and absorbing pressure with a reset that lands in the kitchen.

Drilling is how you make them automatic. These two drills cover the baseline and the transition zone. Run them with a partner, and keep it simple: do each one until you can do it back to back without a mistake.

Drill 1: Baseline drive → drop combo

Change gears in the same rally — hard drive, then soft drop.

Diagram 1 — Drive deep first, then drop soft into the kitchen. Same rally, two very different shots.

Setup: Two players. You start at the baseline, your partner is on the other side at the kitchen and feeds you the ball.

How to run it:

  1. Your partner feeds a ball from the kitchen.
  2. Drive the first ball.
  3. Set your feet before the ball comes back.
  4. Drop the next ball into the kitchen.
  5. Repeat — drive, drop, drive, drop — until you can do it back to back without a mistake.

Two things to get right: Start a little bit behind the baseline, so you have room to step into the drive. Then set your feet before the ball comes back, so you can drop without having to move your feet.

Pro tip — ease up after the drive. After the drive, set your feet on the drop. Don't step in too hard — that is where you pop the drop up. Ease up on the footwork, set, hit the drop, and it will be easier to get to the kitchen.

What success looks like: You can drive and drop back to back, consistently, without a mistake — and a good, set drop makes it easier to get to the kitchen.

Drill 2: Transition-zone resets under pressure

The reset is the hardest shot in pickleball. This is how you practise it.

Diagram 2 — You are a few feet in from the baseline. Absorb the overhead, reset it into the kitchen, and only move up after two good resets in a row.

Setup: Two players. You stand in the transition zone, a few feet in from the baseline. Your partner is at the kitchen, trying to put the pressure on, and starts with a simple overhead.

How to run it:

  1. Your partner hits a simple overhead from the kitchen at you in the transition zone.
  2. Reset the ball into the kitchen and try to play out the point.
  3. Hit at least two good resets from the transition zone in a row before you can move up.
  4. Once you get two good resets, move up toward the kitchen and play out the point.
  5. Repeat from the transition zone.

How to reset under pressure: Stay calm. Stay patient. Keep your paddle out in front, so you are able to reset these balls. Soften your hands to absorb the pace, and keep your feet nice and steady. Use the pressure coming at you — don't fight it.

Resets at pace = next level

If you can consistently put the ball in the kitchen and absorb pressure from the transition zone, your game goes to the next level.

Getting coached teaches you the right mechanics. Drilling is how you make them automatic.
